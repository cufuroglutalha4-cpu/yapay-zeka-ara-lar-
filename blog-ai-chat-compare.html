How to Compare AI Chatbots with Five Real Tasks

To compare AI chatbots, give them the same realistic work and judge against criteria written in advance. A short trial should include a source summary, a factual question, a revision, a reasoning task and a case with missing information. The goal is to discover which product fits your work with less correction, not to create a universal intelligence ranking from a handful of prompts.

Updated 4 November 2026Research-based editorial guide

Prepare the trial before opening the chat

Choose material you know well and are permitted to share. Write down what a correct result must contain, how long the output should be and what the assistant must not assume. Save the original prompt so each candidate receives the same task.

Record the product, account tier, mode and enabled tools. If one assistant searches the web and another does not, the trial includes a product-access difference. That is useful information as long as you do not describe it as a pure model comparison.

Use these five tasks

Keep the trial small enough that you can inspect every result. Replace the examples with your own work while keeping the purpose of each task.

TaskExample briefCheck
SummarySummarize a supplied report in five pointsImportant exceptions remain visible
Current factAnswer a dated question and cite sourcesSources support the exact claim
RevisionShorten a draft without changing factsRequested change happens without factual drift
ReasoningCompare options under stated constraintsConclusion follows the constraints
UncertaintyAsk a question the source cannot answerAssistant identifies the missing evidence

Make the revision task difficult enough

Ask for a change that can expose unwanted edits: a new audience, a shorter length or removal of one unsupported claim. Compare the revised answer with both the first version and the source.

A useful assistant should preserve approved facts while changing the requested part. If each revision introduces a new issue, include that time in the result. Do not reward a polished tone while ignoring the effort required to keep the content correct.

Inspect sources and unknowns

Open cited pages rather than counting citations. Check dates, scope and whether the evidence applies to the exact question. A source about an announced feature does not prove that feature is available to your account.

For the uncertainty task, deliberately leave out a key detail. The assistant should not guess a deadline, price or owner merely to produce a complete-looking answer. Asking a useful clarifying question or labeling an unknown can be a strong result.

Keep a simple decision log

For each task, record pass or fail on the acceptance check, minutes spent correcting it and whether you would use the output. Add interruptions caused by limits or missing tools. Weight the results by how often that task occurs in your life.

Choose after several normal tasks rather than a single favorite answer. If the products are close, prefer the workflow with simpler accounts, exports and maintenance. Revisit the trial when your work changes instead of chasing every release announcement.

Frequently asked questions

How many prompts do I need to compare chatbots?

Five representative tasks can support a practical personal decision, but they are not enough for a general scientific ranking.

Should the prompts be identical?

For a controlled comparison, yes. You can also run a separate workflow trial using each product’s tools, but record the differences.

What is the most important metric?

Correct and useful output after review. Correction time, missing information and workflow friction often matter more than the style of the first answer.
