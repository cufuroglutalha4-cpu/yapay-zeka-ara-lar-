How to Write AI Image Prompts and Revise the Result

A useful AI image prompt describes the asset you need rather than stacking decorative adjectives. Specify the subject, setting, composition, style and delivery constraints, then revise one issue at a time. This updated guide uses current ChatGPT Images and Midjourney workflows; DALL·E references in older tutorials should be treated as legacy context rather than a name for every image created in ChatGPT.

Updated 4 November 2026Research-based editorial guide

Write a brief before writing a prompt

Begin with the image’s purpose. A website banner needs room for a heading; a product illustration needs a recognizable subject; a social thumbnail must remain readable at a small size. These requirements should shape the composition.

Describe the main subject and its relationship to the frame before adding style. Choose one clear visual direction. A prompt that mixes incompatible camera styles, periods and materials can make the result difficult to evaluate because the intended outcome is unclear.

Use a prompt structure you can revise

Keep the prompt organized enough to change one part without rewriting the whole brief. References should be material you are allowed to upload.

PartExample instruction
PurposeA wide editorial banner with empty space on the left
SubjectA compact workshop desk with one open notebook
CompositionDesk in the right half, eye-level view
Lighting and paletteSoft morning light, muted blue and warm wood
ConstraintsNo logos, no extra objects, no embedded heading
DeliveryLandscape image suitable for a website header

Treat the first image as a draft

Check the subject, layout and important constraints before deciding whether you like the style. If the composition is wrong, changing small decorative details will not rescue the asset. Choose the closest useful direction and identify the one most important correction.

Ask for that correction while naming the details to preserve. For example: remove the second notebook, keep the desk angle and lighting, and leave the left side empty. Compare the revision with the prior image to catch unexpected changes.

Use current tools rather than old interface instructions

Midjourney documents image creation and editing on its website, including reference-based workflows. ChatGPT Images supports conversational image creation and revision. Old DALL·E instructions may describe retired controls or a different image system.

Use the provider’s current help for exact buttons and account requirements. Keep your creative brief independent of a specific interface so it remains usable when the product changes. Record the tool and settings with approved assets when repeatability matters.

Finish the asset outside the prompt when necessary

Generated text, product geometry and repeated characters need close inspection. Use a design editor for precise typography and final layout instead of asking the generator to solve every production detail. Export at the intended dimensions and inspect both desktop and mobile crops.

For a series, keep a reference sheet and compare the outputs together. Confirm appropriate usage rights for source material and the service’s terms. A strong prompt improves direction, but it does not turn generated imagery into verified photography or a precise technical drawing.

Frequently asked questions

How long should an image prompt be?

Long enough to specify the subject, composition and important constraints. Extra adjectives are not useful if they make the desired result less clear.

Why does a revision change other parts of the image?

Generation is not always a perfectly local edit. State what must remain, keep previous versions and inspect the result after each change.

Should I still follow a DALL·E tutorial?

Use it for general creative ideas, but verify the workflow. Current ChatGPT image creation is documented as ChatGPT Images, and the official DALL·E GPT has been retired.
